In a hurry?

The separate Quick Start document takes you from installing to a first result in a few minutes. This guide explains every screen and option in detail.

1 · What Tf Studio does

Every glass carries a thermal fingerprint of how it was made, written in its fictive temperature Tf. Tf Studio simulates that history, analyzes your measurements, and fits relaxation models to them.

Tf Studio contains four tools. They open from the home page (web) or from the launcher (desktop app).

ToolUse it to…
Learnunderstand the experiments (annealing, Kovacs jumps, DSC, enthalpy recovery, MDSC) and the models (TNM, MAP / RelaxPy, Takeda–Lucas), with live simulations on every topic.
Labtry a cool–heat scan, an anneal or a T-jump interactively and compare the three models side by side.
Fitterprogram any thermal history, load your data, then compute with chosen parameters or fit the parameters, with uncertainties.
Data Analysisturn raw DSC heat flow into normalized Cp, fictive temperatures and recovered enthalpy, and fit volume (or density, length…) relaxation during annealing.

The three models

ModelIn one line
Takeda–Lucas (TL)Heterogeneous domains, each with its own Vogel temperature and local fictive temperature Tf,i; the spread of Tf,i persists in equilibrium, giving temperature-dependent nonexponentiality and enthalpy fluctuation (J. Chem. Phys. 160, 174504, 2024).
Tool–Narayanaswamy–Moynihan (TNM)One fictive temperature for the whole sample, nonlinearity x and a stretched-exponential (KWW) kernel.
MAP / RelaxPyMauro–Allan–Potuzak nonequilibrium viscosity with Prony-series components (Wilkinson, Mauro & Mauro, SoftwareX 2018).
Your data stay on your computer

All calculations run locally, in your browser or in the desktop app. Nothing you load is uploaded anywhere.

Beta software

Tf Studio is in beta: features, defaults and occasionally results may change between versions. Record the version number (bottom of the launcher) with your results.

2 · Getting Tf Studio

2.1 In the browser

Open wtakeda0129.github.io/tf-studio. Nothing to install; it works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, and always shows the latest version. After the page has loaded, the tools also work offline.

2.2 Desktop app

Download the installer from github.com/Wtakeda0129/tf-studio/releases/latest, or click Download for Mac / Windows on the home page.

macOS (Apple silicon and Intel)

  1. Download Tf-Studio-<version>-universal.dmg.
  2. Open it and drag Tf Studio into Applications (choose Replace if asked).
  3. Open Tf Studio. The first time, macOS says it cannot verify the developer: click Done.
  4. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway, then confirm.

This is needed once per computer, because the app is signed with the group's own certificate rather than an Apple Developer ID.

Windows 10 / 11

  1. Download Tf-Studio-Setup-<version>.exe.
  2. Run it. If Windows shows “Windows protected your PC”, click More info → Run anyway.
  3. Choose the install folder (no administrator rights are needed) and finish.
  4. Start Tf Studio from the Start menu or the desktop shortcut.

2.3 Updates

The desktop app checks for a new version when it starts and every six hours; you can also check with Tf Studio → Check for Updates… (Mac) or Help → Check for Updates… (Windows). A new version downloads in the background and the app offers Restart now to install it.

If macOS cannot install an update in place, the app says so and offers Download, which opens the release page: install the new DMG as in 2.2. The web version needs no updates.

2.4 The launcher

The desktop app opens a launcher with the four tools; each opens in its own window. Shortcuts: ⌘/Ctrl 1 Learn, 2 Lab, 3 Fitter, 4 Data Analysis, ⌘/Ctrl 0 launcher. The version number is shown at the bottom of the launcher.

4 · Learn

Learn is a self-contained course on structural relaxation. The contents on the left jump to any section; Part I covers experiments and Part II the models.

Part I · Experiments

Each experiment has its protocol and equations, then a live simulation of the same example glass, selenium (Tg = 308.13 K, m = 64.14, with the TL parameters of Takeda & Lucas 2024; for MAP the glassy viscosity is Arrhenius with B = x m Tg, x = 0.5, and C = 0):

  • Isothermal annealing: relaxation function φ(t) after a 10 K down-jump, compared with a linear KWW curve, and the apparent KWW parameters.
  • Kovacs experiments: asymmetry of approach (±5 K jumps) and the memory effect (Kovacs; Macedo–Napolitano crossover).
  • DSC on cooling and heating: heating scans after five cooling rates and Moynihan's cooling-rate plot (apparent Δh/R and fragility).
  • Enthalpy recovery: heating scans after annealing and ΔH(ta).
  • MDSC: total, reversing and non-reversing signals, C″, and how the reversing step moves with the modulation period (the Hutchinson and Schawe caveats).

Part II · Models

The mathematics of TNM, MAP and TL, what each assumes, and an interactive comparison of where the fictive temperatures sit.

5 · Lab

The Lab (formerly Explorer) is a quick, always-live playground: change a number and the plots update. Use it to build intuition; use the Fitter for your own histories and data.

  • Models: tick TL, TNM and/or RelaxPy. Material preset loads the TL parameters of Table I of the JCP 2024 paper; Tg and m are shared by the models unless unlinked in their sections.
  • Thermal history: choose a protocol (cool → heat, slow cool, anneal, Kovacs T-jump) or build segments with + Ramp, + Hold, + T-jump.
  • Tabs: Calorimetry (Cp, Tf), Relaxation dynamics, Distributions (TL relaxation-time distributions and βKWW(T)), Time history, Parameters.
  • Run / auto: untick auto to update only when you click Run. Pin keeps a run for comparison; CSV exports the curves.

Steps 1–3 only show the thermal history and your data. The model runs when you click Compute or Run fit in step 4. Any later change clears the old result and asks you to compute again, so a result on screen always matches the current settings.

6.1 Step 1 · Thermal history

  1. Start temperature T₀. The sample starts in equilibrium at T₀ (Tf = T₀). Choose T₀ above Tg.
  2. Insert template (optional). Templates are placed around the current model Tg, so set Tg in step 3 first if your material differs from the default.
  3. Add segments with + Ramp, + Hold / anneal, + T-jump, + MDSC. Reorder, duplicate or delete them with the small buttons on each card.

T-jumptarget TInstantaneous change (e.g. Kovacs experiments).

Under the segments, the summary shows the number of simulation steps and the total time. The right panel plots the temperature program and the time step of every simulation step.

6.2 Step 2 · Experimental data (optional)

  1. Click + Add dataset and choose the Data type (table in section 10).
  2. Choose the Linked history segment: the part of the thermal history the data belong to (e.g. the heating ramp for a DSC heating scan, the hold for annealing data).
  3. Paste / file: paste two or more columns or click Load file…, then pick the x and y columns and units. Or Synthesize from model to generate test data with noise.
  4. Optional: use x ≥ / ≤ to limit the fitted range, a weight, a y multiplier, and fit an offset and a scale factor (y = a + b·model) when the data are not normalized.
  5. Click Add dataset. Tick or untick a dataset to include it; ✎ edits it, × removes it.
Raw data are fine

Types such as “Cp or heat flow vs T”, “Enthalpy vs T” and “property vs t” fit their baselines or linear coefficients exactly at every step, so you can load raw measurements. To normalize DSC data yourself first, use Data Analysis (section 7).

6.3 Step 3 · Model and parameters

  • Choose Takeda–Lucas, TNM or RelaxPy (MAP).
  • Type values. Ticked parameters are free in a fit and move between min and max; unticked ones stay fixed. Bounds around values sets sensible bounds.
  • TL: Preset loads Table I of the JCP 2024 paper; N is the number of domains (200 is accurate). f is fitted on its 0.01 grid.
  • TNM: choose the exact KWW summation or the Prony kernel for display (fits always use Prony).
  • MAP: RelaxPy example glass; A can follow from continuity at Tg, and p = 0.3082·m (RelaxPy default).

6.4 Step 4 · Compute and fit

Compute with chosen parameters

Forward calculation, no fitting. Check or adjust the values and click Compute. With data, the agreement (R², RMSE per dataset) is shown.

Extra results for the TL model

  • Fictive-temperature fluctuation δTf vs temperature, with the equilibrium value.
  • Map of the local fictive temperatures Tf,i vs time, rebinned on a uniform Tf grid (probability density in K⁻¹), with T(t) and ⟨Tf⟩(t).
  • βKWW(T): tick also compute the instantaneous β_KWW(T) (adds about a second).

Fit parameters

  1. Open the Fit parameters tab (needs at least one active dataset and one free parameter).
  2. Choose the Method: Local — Levenberg–Marquardt (fast, starts from the current values) or Global — differential evolution + LM polish (slower, searches the whole box).
  3. Set Max model runs and the Weighting (each dataset equal, or each point equal).
  4. Click Run fit. Stop ends early; Undo fit restores the previous values.

The result lists each parameter with ±1 standard error, flags parameters at bound or poorly determined, and gives R², reduced χ², AIC and BIC. In the correlation matrix, |r| > 0.95 (bold) means two parameters trade off and are not separately determined by your data.

Exports

Export report (.json) (parameters, errors, statistics, history), Model vs data (.csv), Simulation (.csv) (t, T, Tf, τ, τeq, segment), and the SVG button on each plot.

7 · Data Analysis

Data Analysis prepares your own measurements. It has three tabs across the top. Each tab has Load example data (synthetic selenium data made with the TL model, parameters of Se from Takeda & Lucas 2024) so you can try everything first.

7.1 Heat-flow scans

  1. Click + Add run, then choose a file (.csv, .txt, instrument export) or paste columns.
  2. Check the Temperature and Heat-flow columns and their units (K or °C; mW, W/g or arbitrary). Column names like “Temperature (°C)” and “Heat Flow (W/g)” set these automatically.
  3. Set the Sign convention: endothermic up, or endothermic down (exo up) as in TA Instruments exports.
  4. Scan is detected from the temperature trend (heating/cooling). Enter the rate and, for mW, the sample mass.
  5. For a heating scan measured after annealing, enter the annealing time ta and Ta. Leave them empty for the unaged reference.
  6. Click Add run.

Normalization. Enter a glass range and a liquid range (or Auto ranges). A straight line is fitted to the heat flow in each range and extrapolated, giving

CpN = (HF − HFg) / (HFl − HFg) = dTf/dT,    Tf(T) = T* − ∫TT* CpN dT′

with T* at the start of the liquid range (Moynihan's area matching). The card reports the limiting fictive temperature Tf′, the midpoint (CpN = 0.5) and ΔCp at Tf′. Apply to all runs copies the ranges; Export normalized (.csv) saves T, HF, CpN, Tf for every run.

Choosing the ranges
  • Glass range: well below the transition, where the scan is straight; avoid the start-up transient.
  • Liquid range: above the overshoot, where the scan is straight again.
  • For comparisons (cooling rates, aging series), use the same ranges for all runs.

7.2 Enthalpy recovery

  1. Choose the Unaged reference (a heating scan without annealing).
  2. Enter or check the annealing time ta of each annealed heating scan.
  3. Choose how each annealed scan is aligned to the reference: a constant shift in the liquid range, or a straight line through the glass and liquid ranges (use this if the glass lines differ slightly).
  4. Check the integration range (default: from the glass range to the liquid range) and enter Ta.
  5. Optionally override ΔCp (default: the reference at Tf′).

ΔH = (1/q) ∫ ΔHF dT,    Tf = Tf′(ref) − ΔH/ΔCp,    φ = 1 − ΔH/ΔH∞,   ΔH∞ = ΔCp(Tf′ − Ta)

Result columnMeaning
ΔHenthalpy lost during annealing and recovered on heating (J/g with mW + mass or W/g; mJ with mW and no mass)
ΔH/ΔCpthe same in kelvin: the drop of the fictive temperature during annealing
Tf from ΔH / Tf′ (area)fictive temperature after annealing, from ΔH and independently from area matching of the annealed scan (they should agree)
φrelaxation function; with three or more annealing times a KWW fit gives τ, β and ⟨τ⟩
Use the same heating rate

ΔH is computed with the reference's rate. All scans should be heated at the same rate and ideally measured on the same sample.

7.3 Property relaxation

Add a dataset of a property against time during isothermal annealing: volume, density, length, refractive index… (+ Add dataset: pick the time column and unit, the property column, a label and Ta). The fit is

V(t) = V∞ + (V0 − V∞) exp[−(t/τ)β]

with V∞ fitted or fixed at a value you enter (use fixed when the data stop well before equilibrium). The table gives τ, β, ⟨τ⟩ = (τ/β)Γ(1/β), V0, V∞, the relative change and R². Several datasets can be compared on the φ(t) plot.

Systematic residuals (as in Figure 7.4) are expected for real glasses: relaxation is nonlinear, so a single stretched exponential is only an approximation. Fitting the model in the Fitter accounts for this.

7.4 From analysis to fit

Export the normalized curves (tab 1) or φ(t) (tabs 2, 3) as CSV and paste them into Fitter step 2 as “DSC — normalized Cp” or “Annealing — normalized relaxation φ(t)”, linked to the matching segment of a thermal history that reproduces your experiment.

8 · Data files

  • Formats: text files (.csv, .txt, .dat, .tsv) or pasted text. Columns may be separated by commas, semicolons, tabs or spaces.
  • Headers: lines before the numbers (titles, column names, unit rows) are skipped; the last text line before the data is used for column names.
  • Decimal point: use a period (0.25). Files with decimal commas must be converted first.
  • Order: any; cooling scans may run from high to low temperature.
  • Instrument exports: export to text/CSV from the instrument software (e.g. TA Universal Analysis / TRIOS, Netzsch Proteus, Mettler STARe) with temperature and heat flow columns.

9 · Troubleshooting and questions

ProblemWhat to do
The Fitter shows no model curves.Expected: the model runs only when you click Compute or Run fit in step 4.
A plot shows “Could not draw …”.The rest of the page still works. Check the inputs of that part (e.g. a range with fewer than two points) and report it if it persists.
A hold shows no relaxation.The hold is above Tg: the liquid equilibrates within milliseconds. Put the hold below Tg.
Mac: “cannot be opened / cannot verify the developer”.System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway (once per computer).
Mac: “Restart now” does nothing, or the version does not change.Download the latest DMG and replace the app (section 2.2). Versions before 1.1.6 cannot update themselves.
Windows: “Windows protected your PC”.More info → Run anyway (first install only).
TL map or βKWW plot missing.Very long histories (above ~60 000 steps with 200 domains) do not keep every Tf,i. Use a coarser ΔT step or fewer hold points.
A fitted parameter is “at bound”.Widen its bounds in step 3, or fix it if the data cannot determine it.
Parameters strongly correlated (|r| > 0.95).Add data that separate them (several cooling rates, annealing data) or fix one of them.
CpN is upside down.Change the sign convention of the run (endothermic up/down).
ΔH has the wrong sign or size.Check the sign convention, the rate and the sample mass, and that all scans used the same heating rate.

Report problems or ideas at github.com/Wtakeda0129/tf-studio/issues or to the Lucas group.
